Awwwards winner portfolio — UI/UX case study

Robert Mayer
8 min read
·
Mar 8, 2022

--

I was always jealous of people working in studios on various eye-catchy award-worthy websites. Being a part of a team working on a website that wins Canne’s lion legally means that you personally have it in your portfolio. It struck me really hard when I was preparing documents for the O1 visa and realized that it’s kinda hard to prove the product design I did worth something — no shiny trophies or titles involved in that. No way to objectively support my claims.

My experience with studios was rather modest — I spent most of the time working solo or as a product designer. There certainly are awards for product design work, but it’s nothing compared to the opportunities that interactive or promo website has. It’s entirely possible to get a Red Dot, but the effort seems incomparable to getting a CSSDA or Awwwards.

And thus at some point, I decided — I want to become an award-winning designer /s

Portfolio goals

We all know that design is communication. If my portfolio could speak, what should it tell visitors? I was thinking about that a lot. One step above it’s not about the website itself, it’s about branding. What is the image I’m trying to create? What’s the message? I didn’t want to dive too deep into that rabbit hole, but outlined core principles that served me well as a foundation.

Should reflect my UI work to some extent

I don’t think there’s a certain style in all of my works, but definitely, something unites them. I did want my portfolio to be an extension of previous works and not a completely different animal.

Easy to navigate and understand

I wanted to find the right balance between wow-effect and simplicity. I always find a lot of promo websites being too complicated and overstuffed with animations — that was something I wanted to avoid.

The substance has to be louder than the form

This is very important. I realized that for a lot of portfolios (and trust me I’ve seen hundreds) I remember nothing about the designer and his work. I remember transitions, some animations, and stuff — but not the person that website was about. I wanted to avoid that.

Eye-catchy and packed with fancy tech

But even with all that above, I still want to get awwwards, right? So the website has to be stuffed with fun stuff and trendy animations.

This project is also meant to reflect the career changes I’ve been through as a designer. A step up if you like. Essentially I was looking at this portfolio website as an investment in my self-promotion and career.

All these goals dictated a lot of design choices I made. For example, custom-made consistent 3d visuals or images as project covers could look just sweet. But what do they tell about me and my work? It’s about the form and not the substance — something I wanted to clearly draw a line on.

Expenses

Looking ahead I will say this project was hella expensive for a personal pet project. But hey, it is the investment, right? Another year and I would be able to estimate an ROI and I’m 100% sure it is worth every penny.

In total, I spend 150 hours on all things design. Luckily, I was my own boss so no argument on deadlines and budget! Let’s say it’s $100/hour, which gives us $15,000 for a design part of the project.

As I really topped my design dojo on this one, I was expecting developers to be crazily excited to work with me. Maybe even giving me a discount or at least a special treatment. So I made a list of developers who had a track record of awwwards and other interaction trophies and started emailing them.

But the truth is, skilled developers usually have a ton of projects waiting in line for them. And I would guess a lot of them have a massive potential design and style-wise. So… most of the people I contacted hadn't even answered me. I got really lucky that I found the one brilliant developer I was working with afterward, that’s top-level luck right here.

Getting around ten estimates, I've got a $6–14k tag. This may seem like a big number, but given the skillset and experience of these developers, that’s justified.

Here you can stop and say: “Robert, $14k bucks for a personal website, are you nuts? You can make it on a Readymag for $14!” — and yes, for the most part, it’s true. But as I was looking for the best possible quality and wow-effect, I couldn’t afford to cut costs on development. It would be simply against principles I’ve set for this project. It has to be perfect.

So, the rough total cost of this website is $25k — sheesh.

Exploration

I spent a lot of time really just thinking, making notes, and forming that mental image of the final result in my head. As there was no rush I was enjoying that laid-back discovery process which took a lot — half a year or around that.

One of the first mood board assets and style directions I had in mind was retro racing cars aesthetics. Just look at these beauties:

But after a few iterations, I absolutely failed to make anything capturing that style and feel. Well, worth trying anyway!

I was mainly testing ideas, colors, and font combinations on the Hero section. I have to say that the final copy arrived somewhere in the middle of the process, so in the beginning, I was using random people’s /about text as my placeholder. Here are some of the early explorations:

At some point, I realized that I really need some abstract layer to work on. It could be a sentence, some wordplay, or a visual anchor. Something that would make sense and would drive storytelling. I’ve started with “Design ⟼ Impact”, experimented with some other ones, but finally clicked with “Observe. Explore. Create” as it covers the entire design process (and this word combination looks kinda cool).

Well, enough visuals for now, but one last thing I want to show you. I was exploring a lot of options on how I could structure the actual project's grid and one of the options was to use the ‘folders’ concept. I wanted to make it possible to see a range of projects with an option to get enough context with just a bit of information. This is the concept that I finally dropped after a few iterations.

Outcomes

The first and foremost result — I landed a job at the dream company. Essentially, that was one of the main reasons for this project in the first place.

In the few weeks following the launch I’ve received around 40 job invitation — there even was some studios that I was drooling over 4 years ago. Honestly, it was really pleasing to hear all compliments and invitations for a job discussion even if I wasn’t really interested in the proposed position.

As for the awards, here’s what we got:

  • Awwwards Honorable Mention
  • Awwwards Mobile of the Week
  • CSSDA Special Kudos
  • CSSDA SOTD
  • CSS Winner SOTD
  • Fontsninja SOTD
  • FWA Nominee
  • Land-Book Featured
  • Mindsparke Mag SOTD
  • Muzli Frontpage Featured
  • Tympanus Blog Featured

The total number of visitors for the first 3 months is 22,517.

Some reflections

Well, that was fun! I’m very happy with the results even if there are still flaws on the website and parts I’d like to improve. To wrap it up, some of the general realizations I’ve got after this ride:

A perfect pet project with a great creative freedom

A good way to break out from deep product design work. There are times when you just need to flex your creative muscles after spending too much time refining one single component in Figma. it’s really like a breath of fresh air and just the right amount of graphic design work for me.

Way to step up in career and make a statement

Imagine looking through 100 portfolios day after day — that’s a reality for some of the hot companies. There’s high competition and to have a chance in this race we need to stand out. Without a proper portfolio, there’s a high risk of failing at the very first step of getting the best job.

Made me think about my personal brand in a more structured way

I’ve spent an enormous amount of time writing and preparing content and still it’s like 60% of what I planned initially. It’s just a headache to prepare and so many possible ways. At some point, I was working with a Content Marketer to make sure everything is in place.

Thank you

A big thanks to a lot of people for helping me with testing and providing feedback. Your support meant the world for me. And a special thanks to Mario — who made this project live. Hands down the best interaction developer I’ve ever had a chance to work with ❤️
